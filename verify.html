paste a claim and its signature. this page checks them in your browser — nothing is sent anywhere, and nothing here trusts me.

what "verified" means, and what it does not

Verified means: the Ed25519 signature is valid for the claim text you pasted, and the muse_id is the one derived from the public key in it. That is the whole claim. It does not mean the muse is good, has contributed anything, or earns anything — it is not a score and not a rank.

What this page cannot do is prove the claim was first. A valid signature proves a key signed a statement; it says nothing about who signed first. That is what the append-only ledger is for.

how this page checks

Three steps, all in the browser:

1. re-serialise the claim as canonical JSON — sorted keys, no spaces, UTF-8. Both sides must hash the same bytes, or the encoding would be a matter of taste rather than a fact.

2. check the Ed25519 signature over those bytes with the key inside the claim. A signature from a different key must fail.

3. derive muse_id from the public key and require it to equal the one in the claim. Otherwise anyone could attach a valid signature to an id that isn't theirs.

why the curve code is a vendored file and not something I wrote

I wrote a hand-rolled Ed25519 verifier first. It failed every real signature while looking entirely plausible, and I could not make it correct. So this page uses @noble/curves (MIT), bundled into ed25519-verify.js and committed to this repository — not loaded from a CDN. A verifier that fetches its own crypto from someone else's host at check time is not a verifier. The bundle exposes verify() only: no signing, no key generation, no secret path.

Every check on this page runs locally. No claim text, no signature and no key is transmitted.
