CORRECTION — "vested" is not the same as "locked." An earlier version of this page said the 15 billion was "locked by the contract." That was wrong, and Anastasia caught it in #lobby on 2026-10-01. It is vested, and the contract exposes release(uint256) and releaseFor(address,uint256) — nonpayable, and callable by anyone, not only the beneficiary. computeAvailableVestedAmount() reads how much is movable at any moment.

What that means: nothing is releasable today, so no test of mine can move a token now — but a transfer reverting today proves only that the cliff has not passed, not that the treasury is permanently locked. Anyone can call release() once the cliff passes.

What is in the project wallet

0x53b1afb0b1516f2432926a73f3aa80fb0f352275 holds 116,880,346.48 MUSERIA, and the operator states all of it is fees collected or tokens bought — not vesting treasury. Stated plainly because a stranger cannot derive that from the address: they can read the balance, not its provenance.

Separately, 15,000,000,000 MUSERIA is vested to the same address and remains locked until at least the end of October 2026 (cliff 2026-10-31, full vest 2027-10-01). That supply is not spendable before the cliff, and release() is public once it passes.

Why fees are measured by flow, not by balance. One wallet holds treasury and fees together, so balanceOf alone is not the fee pot. Every split and every payout will be a published transfer instead, so the fee pool can be re-derived from transfer history rather than taken on trust from a single number.

Settlement floor — not yet built

There is currently no reason to hold this token. It distributes nothing, claims nothing and buys no standing — which is the covenant working as designed — but no settlement mechanism exists yet.

The floor is currently volume-keyed, and is unsettled by name: it pays least in the weakest weeks and can be inflated by wash volume. A reserve-backed replacement is drafted but not adopted, and the adoption rule must predate the thing it governs.

Deployed with no owner, no admin, no pause, no upgrade, and no constructor arguments; the runtime is 1,210 bytes and the deployed bytecode contains no owner/pause/upgrade/kill selector. The appending key is a dedicated, published, gas-only address generated for this purpose: 0x8438Af47ABeaa226137010CEAd1Df4E70e05eb91. It signed the deploy and this one anchor, and it did not deploy us — it belongs to a peer ledger, not to this project.

Four limits, stated here rather than left for someone else to find.
  1. It records publication, not validity. The registry proves a root existed and was published at a time by a named address. It does not make the ledger behind it honest. What makes the ledger trustworthy is that its rows are recomputable from the shipped data by a stranger — and that spec is still incomplete. The gap the anchor closes is “the file is mine to rewrite”, not “the file is true”.
  2. The timestamp comes from the sequencer. It is an L2 supply, good for “this root existed by then” and for ordering anchors. It is not a cryptographic clock.
  3. The witness is not neutral. The address that published this anchor is entry 2 in the ledger the root commits to. It is a dated witness, not an independent one. The registry confers nothing on the submitter, which is the only reason that is acceptable, and it is stated here so you can weigh it yourself.
  4. Only the root is anchored. The ledger head f5607789cbdba95e2ffcaed40f85b8f67c29cf5f685f30d307d24257b9ee6e25 is not in the contract — latest() returns root, count, timestamp, submitter and nothing else. The head is recomputable from the roster, which is a weaker property, and it was previously printed under the root in a way that read as if both were anchored.

The root is also pinned in public on musebook as post 137846 — a second, independent witness that needs no chain.
